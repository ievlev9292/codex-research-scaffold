# Scientific papers and related materials

Read when editing scientific prose outside visible slides. Apply only the relevant genre guidance.

| Material | Editorial purpose and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Manuscript or section | Make the reasoning readable without supplying missing reasoning. Preserve the roles of methods, results, interpretation, and limitations. Keep attribution, uncertainty, and scope attached to the claims they qualify. Reorder only when dependencies and cross-references remain clear. |
| Abstract | Preserve the question, approach, principal findings, and necessary limitations already present. Compress empty setup and repeated significance. Keep substantive results and caveats; never invent missing results to complete a familiar abstract template. |
| Title | State the supplied subject or finding precisely and compactly. Keep boundaries such as "in simulation" or "under weak driving." Avoid unsupported superlatives and do not convert tentative evidence into a discovery claim. |
| Cover letter | Keep the submission purpose and supported journal relevance, novelty, or contribution. Remove ceremonial praise and generic assurances. A "first" or exclusivity claim is substantive: preserve and flag if unsupported rather than silently changing it. |
| Response to reviewers | Retain the actual agreement, disagreement, explanation, changes made, and commitments. Remove repetitive thanks and canned reassurance; a brief useful acknowledgement may remain. Keep respectful disagreement. Preserve quoted reviewer text exactly and do not promise an analysis or revision the author has not undertaken. |
| Grant or related proposal | Retain an evidence-based case for the question, aims, feasibility, and potential value. Distinguish preliminary results from planned work, and goals from guaranteed outcomes. Trim hype without erasing advocacy or turning a plan into an accomplishment. |

## Handle uncertainty as content

"Consistent with," "may indicate," "we infer," "within uncertainty," and "we cannot exclude" express different commitments. Their apparent repetition may be scientifically necessary. Remove only empty conversational hedges that carry no epistemic meaning. Do not broaden the population, parameter regime, sample, or comparison when shortening a sentence.

## Respect scientific source formats

Keep citations with the propositions they support, including when splitting or joining sentences. Do not alter citation keys, bibliographic entries, equations, labels, cross-reference targets, or exact quotations.

In LaTeX, distinguish editable prose from structural or technical arguments: a section's visible wording may be edited, but a label, citation key, mathematical expression, or macro definition is protected. Use the host's format capability for Word fields, tracked changes, equations, references, and other structured content. Do not implement a substitute parser or claim verification that was not performed.
